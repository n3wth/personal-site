# Passwords and passkeys

Use a different password for every account. Let a password manager remember them. Choose a passkey whenever a service offers one.

You can start with your email today and work through the rest over time.

## Choose a password manager

I use [1Password](https://support.1password.com/explore/get-started/). It saves your logins, creates random passwords, and fills them in for you. Install its app on your phone and computer, plus its browser extension.

You can also use Apple Passwords or [Google Password Manager](https://passwords.google.com/). Pick one that works on the devices you use.

Give the manager a long, unique account password if it asks for one. Follow its recovery setup and keep any emergency kit somewhere safe that you can reach without opening the manager.

## Start with your email

Your email can reset the passwords for many of your other accounts. Protect it first, then your Apple or Google account, banking, and social accounts.

For each account:

1. Open the official app or type the website address yourself.
2. Go to its account or security settings and choose **Change password**.
3. Let your password manager generate a long, random password. Save the change on the website and update the saved login in your manager.
4. Check that the saved login works before moving on.

If you used the old password elsewhere, change it on those accounts too. Adding a number to the end of the same password leaves a predictable pattern.

## Add a passkey when offered

A passkey lets you sign in by unlocking your phone or computer, usually with your face, fingerprint, or device PIN. There is no password to type. It is tied to the real service, which helps protect you from fake sign-in pages.

Look in the account's security settings for **Passkeys** or **Add a passkey**. Follow the prompts and save it in the password manager or device account you intend to keep using. Try signing in with it.

Before replacing a phone, check how you will access your passkeys on the next one. Some sync through your provider; others stay on a single device.

## Keep a way back in

For accounts that still use passwords, turn on two-factor authentication when available. An authenticator app or security key is a good choice. Save any recovery codes somewhere safe, and keep your recovery email and phone number current.

Never share a sign-in code or approve a login you did not start.

## Do I need to keep changing passwords?

Change passwords that are reused, weak, or exposed in a breach. A strong, unique password does not need changing on a calendar.

Changing a password does not normally make your laptop or internet connection faster. This is about protecting your accounts and making sign-in easier.

Further reading: [NIST's password guidance](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/customer/) and [FIDO's guide to passkeys](https://fidoalliance.org/passkeys/).
